Let's be honest. If you go hiking and you meet a bear, you have two options: run and scream, or stay calm, make yourself big, and understand what the bear actually wants.

For the last 2 years, most politicians chose option 1. They run and scream about AI.

"AI will take your jobs! AI is dangerous! We must ban it!"

It's a great story for elections. Fear is cheaper than explanation. And it's easier to scare a hiker than to teach him how to read a map.

But here's the truth from the trail:

An AI agent is not a bear. It's a Sherpa.

The Sherpa doesn't climb for you; he carries the boring weight. When I hike in Kilimanjaro, I don't want my Chaga porters to reach the peak instead of me. I want them to carry the 25kg of water, food, tents and stupid heavy camera batteries, so I can enjoy the view. That's exactly what an AI agent does.

It doesn't do my job.  It carries the 25kg:

- The 27 open tabs I need to compare for one simple decision. 
- The first draft of that email I have rewritten 4 times. 
- The summary of a 2-hour meeting I didn't want to attend.
- The NP-hard map labeling problem that would take me 3 hours of clicking. 

I still decide where we go. It just makes sure I don't die of boredom on the way to base camp.

A politician will never tell you this: "We gave you a Sherpa", because it doesn't win votes, but "A bear is coming to eat you" does.

We already survived this panic 3 times.

- My grandfather, the lead of agriculture, was told the tractors will take the jobs of his workers.
- My mother, accountant, was told Excel will take her job. 
- We were told Google Maps will take the mountain guides' jobs. What happened?

Farmers didn't disappear. They started farming 10x more land. Accountants didn't disappear. They stopped doing stupid addition and started doing actual analysis. Mountain guides didn't disappear. Now they take people to places that were impossible to find before. 
Every time we invented a better backpack, but someone in parliament screamed that we will forget how to walk. We didn't forget how to walk. We just started walking further. AI agents are the lightest backpack ever invented. Lighter than my La Sportiva climbing shoes, and warmer than any Gore-Tex insulated jacket I own.

The real danger is NOT using them. If you are afraid to use an AI agent today because a politician told you to be afraid, in 3 years you will be competing with someone who wasn't afraid. Not with an AI. With a human + AI. It's like going on a winter hike in Caucasus Mountains, climbing Mount Elbrus with canvas sneakers because someone told you that modern boots are "unnatural and dangerous". You can do it. You will just be cold, wet, and slow, while everyone else is already at the hut drinking tea.

Fear doesn't protect jobs. It creates a two-class society: those who have a digital Sherpas, and those who were told Sherpas are evil.

And guess who profits from that? The same people who sell you fear.

So, what to do? Don't ask "Will AI replace me?" That's the wrong question, it's like asking "Will my La Sportiva boots replace me?"

Ask a climber's question:

> What heavy, boring, repetitive weight can I give to my agent tomorrow, so I have more energy for the summit?

For me last Sunday it was: plan a route, check weather, translate 3 articles, and in the evening debug my little autograd toy. My agent did the carrying. I did the thinking. That's not the end of work. That's the beginning of interesting work. Politicians want you scared on the trail. I want you on the summit. 

Your choice!
